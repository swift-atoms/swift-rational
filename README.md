# swift-rational

Use this package when you need an exact fraction: a factor, a quantity or an intermediate result that must never round or overflow, such as a unit conversion factor or a sum of many small parts. Start from `Rational`; wrap it in `Tagged<Tag, Rational>` when the quantity belongs to a domain that must not mix with others. When the fraction converts between two typed domains, use the sibling swift-ratio. When values must round in base ten, use swift-decimal. When an approximate floating-point factor is good enough, use swift-scale.

## Laws

- Every `Rational` is stored in one canonical form: the sign is on `numerator`, `denominator` is positive, the two share no common factor, and zero is 0/1. So equal fractions are `==` and hash alike however they were built (60/120 equals 1/2), and `Rational.zero.polarity` is `nil`. `zero`, `one` and `half` are constants that need no `try`, on `Rational` and on `Tagged<Tag, Rational>`.
- On construction, `polarity: .negative` negates the given numerator, and a negative denominator moves its sign onto the numerator (-6/-8 is 3/4). A zero denominator fails.
- Both components are arbitrary-precision `Integer`s, so `+`, `-`, `*`, `divided(by:)`, `raised(to:)` and `<` are exact and never overflow, however large the components grow. Range is checked only when converting to a fixed-width integer.
- Inverting zero, dividing by zero and raising zero to a negative power fail. A negative exponent inverts first. Anything raised to zero is one, including zero.
- `root(_:)` returns `nil` unless both the reduced numerator and denominator are exact roots of that degree.
- `integer(as:)` checks, in this order: a fraction that is not a whole number is inexact, a negative value into an unsigned type is unrepresentable, and a whole number outside the type's range overflows. `applying(to:)` multiplies exactly first and then converts by the same rules.
- `quotient(dividing:)` divides by the rational as a whole positive divisor and floors: the remainder is never negative and always below the divisor, also for negative dividends (-1 divided by 60 is quotient -1, remainder 59).
- `floor` is the greatest whole number not above the value and `ceiling` the least not below it, for negative values too (-7/2 floors to -4 and its ceiling is -3). Both are exact `Integer`s and are equal exactly when the value is whole.
- `description` prints the numerator alone when the denominator is one and numerator/denominator otherwise, and the string initializer reads every description back as the same value. It also accepts an optionally signed fraction with a sign on either component and a non-zero denominator, and a decimal: an optional sign, one or more digits, and optionally a point followed by one or more digits (`3`, `-12.25`, `+0.50`). A decimal is read exactly as its digits over a power of ten, with no floating point on the way, and never overflows. Everything else is `nil`, including `.5`, `5.`, exponents, a decimal on either side of `/`, more than one `/`, whitespace, `NaN` and `Infinity`.
- `Codable` uses the keys `numerator` and `denominator`. Decoding rejects a denominator that is not positive and reduces the result.
- `approximation(as:)` scales numerator and denominator before converting, so components far beyond the destination's range still give a finite result when their ratio is in range. A result below the destination's normal range comes out as a subnormal rather than as zero. `Double(value)` and `Float(value)` are exactly `value.approximation(as:)` for that type.
- `Rational(exactly:)` is the exact value of a finite binary floating-point number (0.1 as a `Double` is 3602879701896397/36028797018963968), so converting it back gives the same number. Infinity and NaN are unrepresentable. To read a measured value as a fraction, convert it exactly and then round it.
- `rounded(denominator: 120)` is the nearest multiple of 1/120. `rounded(denominator: ...120)` is the nearest fraction whose denominator is at most 120: no fraction with a denominator in that range is closer (π gives 22/7 for `...7` and 355/113 for `...1000`); a value already within the bound comes back unchanged. Both round a tie away from zero, reduce the result, and throw `denominator` for a bound that is not positive.
- `magnitude` is the absolute value. `isFinite` is always `true`.
